A faucet or supply valve that sprays, gushes or will not shut off is an emergency, because the water does not stop until you stop it. Emergency faucet repair in Morton Grove means shutting off the supply, replacing the broken faucet, cartridge or valve and testing for leaks. Licensed plumbers take these calls at any hour. Until one arrives, find the shutoff under the fixture, or the main valve if that one fails.

The shutoff that will not shut off

The worst version of this call is a burst or broken supply valve combined with a main valve that refuses to turn. Old stop valves seize with mineral buildup and corrosion, and some crack when forced. Never use a wrench on a valve that is weeping at the stem, since a hard twist can snap it and turn a drip into a spray.

Start under the sink. Turn the small oval handle clockwise. If it spins without stopping the flow, or it is stuck, go to the main shutoff, usually near the water meter in the basement. Know where it is before you need it, and make sure everyone in the house does too.

Water is flowing and you cannot stop it

  • Shut the main valve for the whole house, turning clockwise until it stops
  • Open one low faucet, such as a basement sink, to drain pressure from the pipes
  • Move belongings out of the wet area and lay towels to slow spreading
  • Keep away from outlets, cords and the electrical panel if water is nearby
  • If the main will not turn at all, call right away and tell dispatch so the plumber brings valve parts
  • Take photos of the damage for your insurer before cleaning up

When a faucet is an emergency and when it is a nuisance

A slow drip at the spout can wait for a daytime visit. A crack in the body, a loose handle that leaks heavily, or a supply line that bursts under the sink cannot. Water in a vanity cabinet soaks the base and spreads under flooring before anyone notices.

Symptoms that point to a faucet or valve problem

  • Water sprays from the base of the faucet or the handle
  • A stop valve under the sink drips at the stem or the nut
  • The handle spins freely and does not control the flow
  • Flow from the spout is weak, uneven or splits sideways
  • The faucet drips after you turn it fully off
  • Hot or cold side makes a banging or hissing noise

Hard water, aerators and worn cartridges

Water here is on the hard side. Evanston's 2023 water report gives an average of about 8.0 grains per gallon, with a range from roughly 7.3 to 8.9. That is enough to leave white crust on spouts and slowly choke the screen inside the aerator, the small mesh disk at the tip of the faucet.

Clogged aerators cause spray that goes sideways and low flow. You can unscrew the tip, soak it in vinegar and brush it clean. If the stream is still weak, or the handle grinds, the cartridge inside is probably worn and a plumber can swap it. Persistent scale across many fixtures points to a water softener installation as the lasting fix.

Which part failed decides the repair

Failed part Typical sign Usual repair
Cartridge or ceramic disk Drips from the spout, loose handle Replace the cartridge, keep the faucet
Supply line Water under the sink Replace the braided line and test
Stop valve Leaks at the stem or will not close Replace the valve, sometimes the pipe stub
Faucet body Cracks, corrosion, leaks at the base Replace the faucet
Aerator or screen Weak, sideways stream Clean or replace the tip

Plumbers weigh age against cost. A faucet over fifteen years old with a leaking body is rarely worth another cartridge. A newer faucet with a worn cartridge is a short job.

Burst or broken supply valve

A broken supply valve is a different job from a drippy spout. The valve may be soldered into copper pipe or threaded onto a stub, and it may feed more than one fixture. The plumber closes a valve upstream, removes the broken one, cuts back any damaged pipe and installs a new quarter-turn valve.

If you suspect water is leaking inside a wall or under a slab as well, a leak detection visit finds it with listening equipment before anything is opened. If a pipe has split, see burst pipe repair.

Shower valves, tub spouts and slow tub drains

A shower valve that drips behind the wall or a showerhead that will not stop leaking is often a worn cartridge, and the fix is replacing it from the front. A tub spout that leaks at the wall may have a failed diverter.

A tub or shower that drains slowly is usually hair and soap near the stopper. Lift the stopper, clear what you find and flush with hot water. If it gurgles or clogs again soon, the blockage sits further down and drain cleaning can reach it.

Fixture changes and village permits

Swapping one faucet for another in the same place is routine work. Relocating plumbing fixtures is different, and the village requires a permit for it. Planning to move a sink, or to put a bar sink where none existed? Get the village building office's answer on permits before anyone starts cutting. The same applies to new fixtures added as part of plumbing installation.

Same-day and after-hours repairs

A late-evening call goes out the same way a morning one does, and the plumber brings common cartridges, supply lines and stop valves. Same-day visits are booked if the day has an opening. Odd or discontinued faucet brands may need a part ordered, in which case the plumber can make the line safe tonight and finish later.

What moves the price of faucet and valve work

  • Part and finish: a basic cartridge costs far less than a designer faucet or a wall-mounted valve
  • Access: a fixture in a tight vanity or behind a tiled wall takes longer to reach
  • Valve condition: a seized stop valve or corroded pipe stub can add parts and labour
  • Number of fixtures: replacing every stop valve in one visit is cheaper per valve than separate trips
  • Time of call: a visit outside weekday hours may be priced differently, and you are told how before work begins
