A toilet that spills onto the floor or never stops running needs the shutoff turned first and a plumber called second.

An overflowing, leaking or endlessly running toilet is a real emergency, and licensed plumbers answer these calls at any hour. Emergency toilet repair in Morton Grove means stopping the water, replacing the part that failed and sealing the fixture so it is safe to use again. While you wait, turn the small valve on the wall behind the bowl clockwise until it stops. Then lift the tank lid and hold the float up if water is still rising.

When a toilet is an emergency and when it can wait

Some toilet problems can sit until morning. Others put water through a ceiling by sunrise. The difference is whether water is leaving the fixture where it should not go.

Call right away if the bowl is rising toward the rim, if water is seeping from the base onto the floor, or if the tank will not stop filling even with the float held up. Those three can ruin flooring and drywall in a few hours. A toilet that flushes weakly but holds its water is fine to leave until a visit later that day.

Signs your toilet needs a plumber today

  • Water climbs to the rim after a flush and does not drain
  • A puddle forms around the base, even a small one
  • The tank refills every few minutes with nobody using it
  • A hissing or trickling sound continues after the flush ends
  • The bowl gurgles when you run the shower or washing machine
  • The toilet rocks on the floor or the bolts feel loose

While you wait for the plumber

  • Close the shutoff valve on the wall or floor behind the toilet by turning it clockwise
  • If the valve is stuck, close the main valve that feeds the whole house
  • Do not flush again, since each flush adds water to a blocked line
  • If the water in the bowl or on the floor looks dirty, treat it as sewage. Send children and pets to another room and put on gloves and boots
  • Put towels at the base and move rugs out of the way
  • If water has reached an outlet or power strip, keep away from it and cut the breaker only when the panel is in a dry spot you can reach safely

Why a running toilet empties your wallet overnight

A toilet that runs after the flush wastes water 24 hours a day, and the cost lands on your next water bill. Morton Grove buys its water from Evanston, and that supply carries chlorine at 0.6 to 1.0 ppm to control microbes, according to the village's 2025 water report. Chlorine is a mild oxidiser, and over the years it dries and curls the rubber flapper at the bottom of the tank, so it stops sealing.

A worn flapper is a quick repair. If the tank is old, the fill valve and the chain are usually worth replacing at the same visit, since they wear on the same schedule. Dropping a few drops of food colouring in the tank is a simple test. If colour shows in the bowl ten minutes later without a flush, the flapper is leaking.

The leak at the floor: wax rings and flanges

Water around the base of the toilet is usually not coming from the tank. It leaks from the seal between the bowl and the drain, called the wax ring, or from a cracked flange. The flange is the ring bolted to the floor that holds the toilet in place and connects it to the drain pipe.

A failed wax ring lets wastewater escape with every flush. You may smell sewer gas, see a stain on the ceiling below, or notice the floor feels soft near the toilet. The plumber lifts the toilet, checks the flange for cracks and rust, replaces the ring and bolts the bowl down again. If the floor under the flange has rotted, the repair grows, and the plumber will tell you before more work starts.

Repair or replace the toilet

Not every toilet should be saved. The table below shows how plumbers usually decide.

Situation Usual call Why
Flapper, fill valve or chain worn Repair Parts are cheap and the bowl is fine
Wax ring failed, flange sound Repair Reseating the bowl fixes the leak
Hairline crack in the bowl or tank Replace Cracks spread and cannot be sealed for good
Constant clogs with a good drain line Replace Older bowls with weak flush channels clog often
Flange cracked or floor soft Repair then rebuild The base has to be solid before any bowl goes back

Replacing a toilet that cannot be saved

If the bowl is cracked or fails every few months, replacement is the better value. A plumber removes the old fixture, repairs the flange, sets a new wax ring and checks for leaks before leaving. The job is the same kind of work covered under plumbing installation, including a new shutoff valve and supply line.

Moving a toilet to a different spot is a bigger matter. The village requires a permit for relocating plumbing fixtures, so a straight swap at the same position is simple, while a new position needs paperwork first.

When the toilet is blocked, not broken

A toilet that backs up is not always a toilet problem. If the bowl clears but the tub or floor drain gurgles, the blockage sits in the branch line or the main drain. A plunger will not fix that, and a closet auger only reaches the trap.

Use the auger or plunger once or twice if only one toilet is slow. If the problem repeats within weeks or hits several fixtures, the line needs drain cleaning. Roots and heavy buildup in the main may call for sewer line cleaning, and the plumber can explain which one fits what the fixtures are doing.

What drives the cost of a toilet repair

What changes the price of toilet work

  • Part that failed: a flapper or fill valve is a small repair, a new bowl or a rebuilt flange is a larger one
  • Floor and flange condition: a rotted subfloor adds carpentry before the toilet can be set again
  • Fixture choice: basic bowls and taller, water-saving models are priced differently
  • Shutoff and supply parts: a seized valve under the tank may need replacing so the job can finish
  • Time of call: a late-night or Sunday visit may be priced differently, and the plumber states that before starting
