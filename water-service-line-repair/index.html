When the pipe between the street and your meter fails, you need a licensed plumber who knows the permit, the dig and the lead question.

Emergency water line repair in Morton Grove covers the buried pipe that brings water from the street main to your meter and on into the house. When that pipe leaks or breaks, you may see a soggy lawn, a drop in pressure, or a meter that spins with every tap off. Licensed plumbers go out on 24/7 dispatch, plus same-day visits where the schedule allows.

If water is surfacing or the house has lost pressure, close the main valve inside the house and call. If water is flooding the street, call the village instead.

While you wait for the plumber

  • Turn the main shutoff valve inside the house to closed, which keeps water from reaching the line.
  • Keep children and pets away from any pooled water or soft, sunken ground.
  • Do not dig on your own. Buried gas, electric and communications lines may run close by.
  • Fill a few containers with drinking water if the line is fully out.
  • Note the meter reading and photograph the wet area for later.

When a buried water line is an emergency

A service line that has split can pour water into the soil for hours before it shows on the surface. When it does, the soil may sink, the lawn may squelch, or the basement wall may seep. A line that fails completely leaves the house with no water at all.

Treat it as an emergency if water is flowing, if the ground near the foundation is wet and spreading, or if the house has no water. A small steady loss that you can stop at the main valve can often wait for a same-day visit.

Symptoms of a water service line leak

  • Pressure that dropped across the whole house, not at one tap
  • A meter that keeps running with every fixture turned off
  • A wet strip, soft spot or sunken patch of soil between the street and the house
  • Brown or cloudy water that appears after pressure loss
  • The sound of running water outside the house or near the meter
  • A water bill that climbed although daily habits stayed the same

Water service line leak: finding the break

The service line is out of sight, so the first job is locating the leak and not digging a trench the length of the yard. The plumber starts by confirming that the loss is on your side of the meter, using the meter test and a pressure check.

Next comes listening gear on the shutoff and the meter, and a line locator that traces the pipe's path and depth. For quiet leaks, tracer gas pushed into the line shows where it escapes through the soil. If you want a closer look at those methods, the leak detection page covers them in more detail.

Once the break is pinpointed, the dig can be sized to the repair. A spot repair with a short trench costs less to restore than a long open cut, and the old line can sometimes be joined to new pipe at the break.

Underground line leaks and repair options

When a buried line is damaged, there are two main choices. A spot repair replaces the failed length and joins it to the sound pipe on each side. A full line replacement swaps the whole run from the main to the meter.

Option When it fits Digging Honest trade-off
Spot repair One clean break in an otherwise healthy line Short trench at the break Less disruption, but the next failure may be nearby on an old line
Full replacement Corroded pipe, multiple leaks, or lead or galvanized material Open trench or trenchless method where the soil and layout allow it Costs more up front, and removes the weak spots in one visit
Partial replacement Healthy village side, failing private side Trench on the private side only Leaves the older material in place on the other segment

Trenchless methods such as pipe pulling or bursting can reduce lawn and driveway damage, but they need the right soil, a clear path and access pits at each end. The plumber explains whether the method suits your line before you decide.

Lead service lines and what to ask before the dig

Some older water service lines were made of lead. Before anyone digs, find out what your line is made of. The village's online inventory of lead service lines can be searched by address. We do not say which homes have lead lines, because that inventory is the source. Public Works at (847) 470-5235 can also look up your address.

This matters when a line is leaking, because a repair is the moment to decide whether to replace more of it. If the line is lead, a spot repair may leave lead pipe in service. A full replacement removes the question.

The village's 2024 lead testing found a 90th percentile result of 12.4 ppb, with 3 sample sites over the 15 ppb action level. That is a village-wide sample, not a reading for your house. The village report suggests letting the cold tap run for a few minutes before you drink, and choosing a filter certified to ANSI standards if lead is a worry. For drinking water questions, the EPA hotline is 1-800-426-4791.

Permits, 811 and who does what

A water line repair triggers a village permit, the same as a sewer repair does. A homeowner does not have to know the forms. The plumber confirms what applies, and anything the village has not published is a question for the Inspectional Services desk at (847) 470-5214.

Illinois law requires a call to JULIE at 811 before digging, and marking takes time. The call protects the gas, electric and communications lines that run through parkways and yards. In a true emergency the plumber works with the stop-water steps first, then arranges the dig when the markings are in place.

Digging contractors need a Village Contractor's License, and you can ask to see it. State licensing is a separate check. The IDPH lookup lists plumber licenses, which start with 058-, and plumbing contractor registrations, which start with 055-.

Low water pressure that is not the leak

Low pressure does not always mean a broken line. A partly closed main valve, a failing pressure regulator, a clogged aerator or a bad fixture cartridge can give you a weak flow at one tap or several.

Evanston's 2023 water report shows Morton Grove's source water at about 8 grains per gallon of hardness, which is moderately hard. Mineral scale builds up over time inside older pipe and in fixture screens, and it narrows the flow. If scale is the cause, a water softener installation can slow the buildup, though it will not fix a leak.

Pressure that fell suddenly across the whole house, with a wet patch outside, points back to the service line. Pressure that dropped slowly over years points to scale or old galvanized pipe, where a repiping conversation is more honest than another repair.

Leak or break inside the house

A burst section inside the house is handled differently from a buried line. If water is spraying from a wall or ceiling, turn off the main and read our burst pipe repair page for the fix. The same licensed plumbers handle both jobs.

What affects the cost of water line repair

  • Depth and length: A shallow spot repair is quicker than a deep or long run.
  • Surface above the pipe: Lawn, driveway, sidewalk and landscaping each add restoration work.
  • Pipe material: Lead, galvanized, copper or plastic lines need different fittings and handling.
  • Method: Open trench and trenchless methods differ in equipment and site preparation.
  • Permits and utility marking: The permit, the 811 wait and any inspection are part of the schedule.

Home insurance policies vary. Some cover water damage inside the house from a sudden break but treat the underground line as an exclusion unless you added a service line rider. Check your policy early and keep the plumber's written findings. A firm price is given before the trench is opened.
