When a plumbing problem cannot wait

An emergency plumber is a licensed plumber who responds to problems that damage your home or put people at risk if they sit overnight. Our dispatch line for Morton Grove is open around the clock, so a pipe that splits at 3 a.m. gets the same attention as one at noon. Call (847) 281-8626 and describe what you see.

Some problems are clearly emergencies, and some only feel like one. These are the ones that should not wait for morning:

  • Water spraying or pouring from a pipe, ceiling or wall
  • Sewage or dirty water coming up through a floor drain, tub or toilet
  • No water at all to the whole house
  • A water heater leaking from the tank or hissing near the relief valve
  • Water standing in a basement near the furnace, water heater or electrical panel

A dripping faucet, a slow drain in one sink or a running toilet can usually wait for a same-day appointment when available. If you are not sure which group you are in, call and ask. The dispatcher will help you decide.

What to do while you wait for the plumber

Gas smell: leave the building right away, call 911 and then the gas emergency number on your Nicor Gas bill. Do not flip switches or use a phone inside.

  • Shut off the water. Close the main shut-off valve, usually near the water meter or where the line enters the basement. Turn it clockwise until it stops.
  • Stop using water. Do not flush toilets or run sinks, which can push more water or sewage into the problem.
  • Stay safe. Keep away from standing water near outlets or the panel. Shut off power only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water. Avoid contact with sewage.

What makes plumbing in Morton Grove different

Morton Grove buys its water from the City of Evanston, and it reaches the village through the Morton Grove/Niles Water Commission. The source is Lake Michigan. Evanston's 2023 water report shows average hardness of 136 mg/L, about 8.0 grains per gallon, which is moderately hard. That is enough for scale to build on heating elements and the bottom of a tank over the years, so a tank that is never flushed tends to run noisy and lose efficiency.

The village report lists chlorine at up to 1.0 ppm as a microbe control. A faint chlorine smell from the tap is normal for treated lake water and is not a pipe problem. A smell like sewer gas points to a dry trap or a drain fault, and a plumber should look.

The sewers are the bigger story. The village has both combined sewers, which carry sewage and stormwater together, and separate sanitary sewers. About 22.7 miles are still combined, mostly in the northeastern part of the village.

In a heavy rain, those lines fill fast, so a basement floor drain in that area is where a backup shows first. If you live there, a backwater valve or overhead sewer is worth pricing before the next storm, not after.

The village grew from 2,010 residents in 1940 to 20,533 in 1960, so many homes are 60 or more years old. The village's own flood program targets homes built before 1965 because their gravity sewer connections are more likely to back up into the basement in long, heavy rain. If your home is from that era, ask for a sewer line inspection to see what the pipes below the slab look like, and look into the program described below.

Permits and licences before the work begins

Morton Grove note

Morton Grove Building and Inspectional Services requires a permit for water heaters, sewer and water line repairs, relocating plumbing fixtures, and installing sewer cleanouts, flood control valves and overhead sewers. Water heater permits can be applied for online. Normal plumbing maintenance and power rodding of sewer lines do not need a permit.

Any contractor working in the village needs a Village Contractor's License, which requires liability insurance and a surety bond and is valid for 12 months. For permit questions beyond this, call the Building department at (847) 470-5214 at Village Hall, 6101 Capulina Ave.

To check any plumber, use the online license verification tool from the Illinois Department of Public Health. Plumber licence numbers begin 058-, plumbing contractor registrations begin 055- and apprentice licences begin 056-.

Where we send plumbers around Morton Grove

Calls from Morton Grove (ZIP 60053) come in at any hour, and the same dispatch line covers the neighboring towns. The Niles ZIP is 60714, and Niles shares the water commission with Morton Grove, so water quality advice carries over. Skokie has two ZIPs, 60076 and 60077, and its Skokie water comes from Evanston as well, which means the same moderate hardness.

Glenview (60025 and 60026) buys its water from Wilmette instead, so its numbers differ a little. Lincolnwood uses 60712 and sits on the Skokie and Chicago border. Park Ridge uses 60068 and buys water from the City of Chicago, a different supplier than Morton Grove, which is worth knowing if a water heater warranty or filter question comes up.

Planned work keeps emergencies away

Most emergencies give a warning first. A tank often weeps for weeks before it fails, and the rating plate on the side shows its build date. If it is old, book a water heater replacement on a day you choose rather than a night the tank floods the basement. A tankless unit is another choice, and the tankless water heater page covers the trade-offs.

Test a sump pump before the wet season. Pour water into the pit, watch the float lift and the pump run, and check that the discharge line drains away from the house. If it fails, a sump pump installation or repair visit costs far less than a flooded basement. A camera run through the main line, a sewer inspection, shows roots or sags before they cause a backup.

The village offers a loan for homes built before 1965 to convert gravity sewer connections so sewage cannot back up. It is owner-occupied homes only, one time per address, and funding changes by year, so confirm current status with Public Works. For that work, an overhead sewer or exterior backflow valve is installed by an Illinois-licensed contractor, and the program asks for three written estimates.
