Jetting for stubborn lines

Emergency Hydro Jetting in Morton Grove, IL

When a snake only punches a hole and the clog returns, high-pressure water scours the whole pipe wall clean.

24/7 Emergency Line Open
Call (847) 281-8626

If a plumber snaked your line and the cable got stuck, or the drain clogged again a few weeks later, the pipe probably needs more than a hole poked through it. Emergency hydro jetting in Morton Grove is the use of high-pressure water, sent through a hose with a special nozzle, to cut away grease, scale and roots and flush them out of a drain or sewer line.

Dispatch is staffed around the clock and sends licensed plumbers when a line is fully stopped. While you wait, keep water use to a minimum.

Jetting is not the first tool for every clog. It is the right one when the problem is on the pipe wall, not in the middle of the pipe.

Signs a line needs jetting

  • A cable went in, but it snagged and would not pass
  • The drain ran fine for a few weeks, then slowed again
  • Gurgling in more than one fixture after a cable visit
  • Roots found in the line during an earlier camera look
  • Grease buildup in a kitchen or restaurant-style drain line
  • A main line that backs up whenever the washer drains

What high-pressure water does that a cable cannot

A cable cuts a channel through a blockage. The pipe wall stays coated, so the channel narrows again as new debris collects. A jetting nozzle fires water backward and sideways at high pressure, which spins and scrubs the full inside surface as the hose is pulled back toward the cleanout.

The result is a pipe that is close to its original diameter. For grease, scale and soft root growth, that clean wall is what stops the next clog from forming. The plumber starts with a lower pressure and a smaller nozzle on old or fragile pipe, and only raises it if the line holds up.

When a stopped main line cannot wait

An urgent call means the main line is fully blocked, the basement floor drain is bubbling, or water is backing into the tub. In those cases the plumber may start with a cable to open the line and jet it in the same visit. A daytime visit the same day is possible when the schedule allows, and calls at night reach the same dispatch desk.

Before the plumber arrives

Treat any water coming up from a floor drain as sewage.

  • Stop using sinks, tubs, the washer and the dishwasher. Do not flush.
  • Keep children and pets out of the wet area and avoid touching the water.
  • Lift stored boxes, rugs and cords off the floor.
  • Do not pour drain chemicals. They can splash back when a jetting hose is run.
  • Keep away from outlets and the panel if water has spread toward them. Touch the breakers only from a dry, clear floor.

Snaked it and it got stuck

A cable that jams usually has found something solid. It may be a thick root mass, a collapsed section of old pipe, or a built-up wall of grease that the cable cannot cut. Pushing harder can break the cable or pack the debris tighter.

If this happened to you, say so on the call. A plumber who knows the cable stuck will plan a camera look before jetting, because high-pressure water against a broken pipe can push soil into the line or wash out the ground around it. Seeing the pipe first is the safer way to decide.

Clogs that come back weeks later

A line that clears and then fails again on a timetable is usually coated with buildup. Cooking fat cools into a hard layer. Mineral scale grows on the walls of older pipe. Tree roots find a tiny joint, enter as hairs and thicken year after year.

Jetting removes the layer, but it does not repair a cracked joint where roots enter. A camera look afterward tells you whether the line is clean and sound or needs sewer line repair to keep roots out. Plenty of homes choose jetting on a yearly or two-year schedule if the pipe is otherwise in good shape.

Root intrusion in house lines

Roots seek water and oxygen. A small crack or loose joint in a clay or cast iron lateral lets in both. Jetting with a root-cutting nozzle shreds the roots inside the pipe and flushes them downstream.

Cut roots grow back unless the opening is sealed. That is why the choice often comes down to a repeat service plan versus a repair, and why a camera record helps you compare both options. For lines that clog less often, an ordinary drain cleaning visit may be enough.

What a jetting visit costs

Price depends on the line and the problem rather than on one standard figure. A clear price before work starts lets you compare it with the cable-only option.

What changes the price

  • Line length and size: a long run from the house to the street takes more hose and more time.
  • Access point: a clean basement cleanout is quick. Working through a toilet flange or roof vent takes longer.
  • Blockage type: soft grease clears quickly, dense root mass needs extra passes and a cutting nozzle.
  • Camera work: a look before and after adds time but shows whether the line is sound.
  • After-hours dispatch: a night or weekend call costs more than a daytime appointment.

Insurance rarely pays for jetting because it counts as maintenance. If a backup has damaged floors or belongings, a few policies cover that damage through a separate sewer backup rider, so ask your insurer before you decide anything.

Combined sewers and house-line roots

In the northeast corner of the village, the sewers are combined and carry sewage together with storm runoff. Heavy rain can slow flow in those village mains, and a partly coated house line then backs up sooner than a clean one. Whatever the neighborhood, roots and grease are problems inside your own lateral, which you own. Jetting keeps your side of the connection clear. See sewer line cleaning if the whole line is backing up.

Morton Grove note: jetting and permits

The village lists power rodding of sewer lines as work that needs no permit. Hydro jetting is a similar maintenance task, so the same logic applies, but a call to (847) 470-5214 settles any doubt. The permit requirement starts when a line is actually repaired, as with a root-damaged joint.
