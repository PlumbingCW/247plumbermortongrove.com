Cost for Sewer Line Repair in Morton Grove, IL: A Price Guide

Spot repair, lining and full replacement are priced very differently. See what drives each one and how to compare bids.

By the Morton Grove 24/7 Plumbing team. Published October 15, 2026. Updated October 15, 2026.

Call (847) 281-8626

Sewer line repair cost in Morton Grove, IL comes down to what is wrong with the pipe, where the damage sits, how deep it is, and which repair method fits. A short spot repair costs far less than replacing the whole line, but only a camera inspection can tell you which one you need. No honest price exists before that look.

This guide does not list dollar figures, because they would mislead you. It explains the factors that move a bid, compares the three main repair types, and describes a village loan that can help some Morton Grove homeowners pay for backflow protection.

Camera first, quote second

Before any plumber prices a sewer repair, the line should be inspected with a camera. A small camera on a flexible cable travels through the pipe and shows cracks, roots, offsets, collapsed sections and sags where water pools. The plumber marks the distance and depth of each problem.

This step turns a vague estimate into a defined scope. Without it, a bid is a guess, and a guess tends to be padded or short. Ask for a copy of the recording or at least still images of each defect. Our sewer line repair page describes what that process looks like, and the sewer inspection page covers the camera work in detail.

Signs your sewer line may need repair

  • Slow drains in several fixtures at once, especially on the lowest floor.
  • Gurgling from toilets or floor drains when water runs elsewhere.
  • Sewage smell in the yard or basement.
  • Soggy or sunken patches of lawn along the line.
  • Repeat clogs a few months after each cleaning.
  • Backups in the basement floor drain after heavy rain.

If these are present, a cleaning might clear the clog but not fix a cracked pipe. The camera shows which you have.

Spot repair, lining or replacement

The right repair depends on the condition of the pipe and what is on top of it. Here is how the main choices differ.

Method What happens Best when Limits
Spot repair A short damaged section is dug up and replaced One isolated break or offset Does not fix other weak spots
Pipe lining A resin liner is cured inside the old pipe Pipe is cracked but still in shape Needs a clear line, may reduce diameter slightly
Pipe bursting New pipe is pulled in as the old pipe is broken apart Long runs with limited digging Needs entry and exit pits
Full replacement by trench The old line is dug out and replaced Pipe that has collapsed, sagged badly or filled with roots Most disruption to yard and driveway

None of these is always the best. A spot repair can be the smartest choice when the rest of the pipe is sound. A liner can save a lawn or driveway but cannot fix a collapsed section. Ask your plumber why a method fits your line and what would change the answer.

Why depth and length matter

A shallow pipe is quick to reach. A deep pipe needs more digging, shoring and time. Length matters because each extra foot is more pipe, more labor and a larger area to restore afterward. Under a driveway, patio or tree roots, the job gets slower and the restoration costs more.

What the plumber finds when opening up

Even with a camera, surprises happen. A line may be in worse shape than the images showed, or have a second break nearby. A fair bid explains how changes will be handled before work starts, so you are not surprised by a line item mid-job.

What moves the price

Factors that move a sewer line repair price

  • Repair method: A spot repair, a liner, bursting and trench replacement have different equipment and labor needs.
  • Length of pipe affected: More feet means more material and time.
  • Depth and location: Deep lines, lines under concrete or beside trees take longer and cost more to restore.
  • Access: A cleanout speeds the work. A missing cleanout may need to be installed.
  • Permit and inspection: replacing or repairing a sewer pipe requires a village permit, plus an inspection.
  • Restoration: Lawn, concrete and landscaping repair are often separate from the plumbing.

Homeowners insurance often excludes sewer lines that fail from age or roots, though some policies offer an endorsement for service lines. Check yours before a problem arises.

Getting three written estimates

For sewer work, comparing bids is the best protection. Ask each contractor for a written scope that names the method, the footage, the restoration and the permit. Then check that each plumber holds a valid license through the IDPH online tool.

Be wary of a bid that skips the camera, or one that differs wildly from the others without explanation. A low bid that leaves out the permit, the cleanout or the restoration is not a real saving. The same warning applies to a high bid that bundles in work you did not ask for.

Cost of the wrong fix

Repeated cleanings on a broken pipe cost money and keep the underlying problem alive. If a camera shows a cracked or collapsed section, cleaning only clears the symptom. On the other side, replacing a line that only needed cleaning is also waste. That is why inspection comes first.

If your line is clogged with roots but the pipe is intact, cutting or hydro jetting may be all it needs. Roots will return unless the entry point is repaired, so ask what the plan is for the next few years.

Village help for overhead sewers

Some Morton Grove homeowners can get help paying for sewer backup protection. This is not a repair of the main line to the street, but it can matter if your concern is basements flooding from the sewer.

Morton Grove note

Owner-occupied houses built before 1965 inside the village can apply to its overhead sewer assistance program. The money goes toward changing how the house drains so sewage cannot climb back in. One route is an overhead sewer, where the soil stack is reworked and nothing below the basement slab drains by gravity. The other route is a valve installed outdoors on the lateral, with a pump housed in an underground vault.

The loan pays 50% of the real cost, capped at $5,000. If you hire a plumber whose bid is higher than the lowest one, the 50% is figured on the lowest bid. No interest is charged unless the balance is missed when due. Repayment comes at sale or on the 10-year anniversary, and each address can borrow once.

An application needs bids in writing from three contractors holding Illinois licenses. Separately, the village code (Municipal Code 7-5-4A) bars roof, foundation and stairwell drainage from tying into the sanitary sewer.

Funds are awarded in the order requests arrive and the budget may differ each year, so call Public Works on (847) 470-5235 to learn what is open today. Repairs to a sewer or water line need a permit, and a village-issued contractor credential is expected from whoever does the work.
