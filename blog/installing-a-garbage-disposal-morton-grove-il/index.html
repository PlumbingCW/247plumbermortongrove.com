Disposal install guide

Installing an InSinkErator Garbage Disposal in Morton Grove, IL

Mounting ring, drain, dishwasher knockout and wiring each take their own step, and one missed plug causes the classic flood.

By the Morton Grove 24/7 Plumbing team. Published October 15, 2026. Updated October 15, 2026.

Call (847) 281-8626

Installing a garbage disposal in Morton Grove, IL means removing the old unit or sink flange, mounting a new disposal under the sink drain, connecting it to the trap and dishwasher, wiring it to power, and testing for leaks. An InSinkErator-style disposal is built to bolt up in a few clear stages. A handy homeowner can do it in a few hours, but the wiring and the drain connection are the parts worth double-checking.

This guide covers the tools, the steps, and the places where installs go wrong. If you would rather have a plumber do it, call (847) 281-8626. The line is staffed at all hours, and a same-day visit is often available depending on the schedule.

What to gather first

A disposal install uses few tools, but missing one halfway through is a nuisance. Collect these before you start:

  • A flat-blade screwdriver and a Phillips screwdriver
  • Pliers and an adjustable wrench
  • Plumber's putty
  • A hacksaw or tubing cutter for the drain pipe
  • A bucket and rags
  • Wire strippers and wire nuts
  • A disposal power cord kit if the unit does not come with one

Choose a disposal with the horsepower and mount style that suit your sink. A quick-lock mount makes later swaps simple. Check that the new unit fits the space under your sink, since a deep unit can bump into a shelf or the dishwasher hose.

Before you cut anything: power and water

Turn off the power to the disposal circuit at the breaker, and confirm it is dead with a voltage tester. Remove the old unit's wiring cover and check again. Never trust a wall switch alone, because the switch may be on the wrong side of the circuit.

Then clear the cabinet under the sink. Set a bucket under the trap, since water in the trap will spill when you disconnect it.

Wet electrics

If water is on the floor near the disposal or the outlet, stay clear. Flip the breaker only when the floor by the panel is dry and you can reach it safely. Otherwise leave the area and call a plumber or an electrician.

Step by step

Installing the disposal

  1. 1
    Remove the old unitloosen the mounting ring and lower the old disposal. Disconnect the drain elbow, the dishwasher hose and the wiring.
  2. 2
    Clean the sink openingscrape off old putty from around the sink hole. A clean surface helps the new flange seal.
  3. 3
    Set the sink flangeroll a rope of plumber's putty and press it around the underside of the flange lip. Seat the flange in the sink hole.
  4. 4
    Build the mounting assemblyslide the gasket, backup ring and mounting ring onto the flange from below. Fit the snap ring into its groove.
  5. 5
    Tighten the screwsturn the three mounting screws in alternating steps until the assembly is snug and the putty squeezes out evenly.
  6. 6
    Prepare the disposalremove the cover plate on the unit. Knock out the dishwasher plug if you have a dishwasher.
  7. 7
    Wire the unitconnect black to black, white to white and green to the ground screw, then replace the cover plate.
  8. 8
    Hang the unitlift the disposal to the mounting ring, line up the tabs and twist it into place.
  9. 9
    Connect the drainattach the discharge elbow with its gasket, then connect it to the trap with a trap adapter.
  10. 10
    Connect the dishwasher hoseslide the hose onto the inlet nipple and secure with a clamp.

Work slowly on the mounting step. Uneven tightening gives a leak at the flange that is hard to fix later.

The dishwasher knockout plug

The knockout plug is the small disc inside the dishwasher inlet on the side of the disposal. If you connect the dishwasher hose without knocking out the plug, the dishwasher cannot drain. Water backs up into the dishwasher or the sink, and the mess tends to appear after the first full cycle.

Use a screwdriver and a hammer to tap the plug through. Then fish the plug out of the disposal chamber with pliers or tongs. A plug left inside can jam the grinding wheel on first use. If you have no dishwasher, leave the plug in place.

Dishwasher drain loop

The hose should loop up high under the counter before it reaches the disposal. That loop, or a proper air gap where required, helps prevent dirty water from flowing back into the dishwasher. Check how your home's dishwasher is set up and keep the loop in place after the install.

Trap and drain connection

The drain side of the disposal needs a trap, a U-shaped section of pipe that holds water and blocks sewer gas. The trap sits below the disposal, and the pipe slopes down to the wall. Gentle slope matters because flat or backward-pitched drain pipe collects food waste.

Use plastic or metal slip-joint fittings with fresh washers. Hand-tighten, then give a small turn with pliers. Overtightening cracks plastic nuts, and a loose nut leaks.

Wiring and power

Most disposals connect to a switch on the wall through a short cable and a hard-wired connection or a plug. If you are not comfortable working with household wiring, hire an electrician. A mistake with electricity near a wet sink is dangerous.

The village adopted the 2011 National Electrical Code, so wiring in Morton Grove should follow it. In practice, the disposal needs a grounded circuit, a proper cable clamp where the cable enters the unit, and a cover plate that stays on. If your kitchen outlet needs a new circuit or has no ground, call an electrician before you install.

Test run and first-use checks

Restore power. Run cold water into the sink and turn on the disposal for a few seconds. Look under the sink for drips at the flange, the discharge elbow, the trap and the dishwasher hose.

If everything stays dry, run the dishwasher briefly and check again. A slow drip that you only see with a dry paper towel is still a leak, and it will worsen with time.

Break-in habits

Feed the disposal soft food scraps in small amounts, with cold water running. Avoid grease, bones, fibrous stalks such as celery in big handfuls, and coffee grounds in quantity. Cold water helps solid fats move through the pipe before they harden. If the drain slows down after installation, our drain cleaning service can clear the line.

When to call a plumber

Some installs are best left to a licensed plumber. Call if the old sink flange is stuck, if the trap arm does not line up, if the drain pipe in the wall is corroded, or if you have no experienced hand for the wiring. The same goes for a unit that jams or hums after install, which our garbage disposal repair page covers.
