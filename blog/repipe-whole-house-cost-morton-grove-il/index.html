Repipe Whole House Cost in Morton Grove, IL: A Price Guide

What a whole-house repipe costs depends on six things you can see before the quote. Here is how to read each one.

By the Morton Grove 24/7 Plumbing team. Published October 15, 2026. Updated October 15, 2026.

Call (847) 281-8626

The repipe whole house cost in Morton Grove, IL is set by the number of fixtures, the pipe material, how much drywall must be opened, and how the work is scheduled. A repipe replaces the supply pipes that bring water to every faucet, shower, toilet and appliance. It does not replace the drain pipes that carry waste out.

We do not quote dollar figures in an article, because a figure without a visit would be a guess. What we can do is show you each factor, so that when quotes arrive you can compare them fairly and spot what is missing.

What a whole-house repipe actually includes

A repipe swaps the hot and cold water lines inside the house, from the point where water enters to every fixture. A licensed plumber shuts off the water, cuts out old pipe, runs new lines and reconnects each fixture. Then the system is pressure tested before the walls are closed.

The word "whole-house" matters. Some quotes cover only the lines inside the walls. Others include the shutoff valves, the water heater connections, and the pipe between the meter and the house. Ask in writing which of these are in the price, since leaving one out can change the comparison. The main water line between the street and the house is its own scope, covered under water service line repair.

Signs a house may need a repipe

  • Rust-colored or brown water when you first open a tap.
  • Low pressure at several fixtures, not just one.
  • Repeated pinhole leaks in different spots.
  • Green stains or crust on copper, or flaking on galvanized steel.
  • Metallic taste or a cloudy look to hot water.
  • A plumber finds pipe walls thinned or corroded during a repair.

One leak in an otherwise sound system is a repair, not a repipe. Several leaks in different places is a pattern.

The six factors that set the price

Most of what moves a repipe quote falls into these categories. If a quote does not address them, ask.

What moves a whole-house repipe price

  • Number of fixtures: Each sink, toilet, shower, tub and appliance connection needs its own supply run and shutoff.
  • House size and layout: Two stories, a finished basement or a long distance between the water heater and the bathrooms add pipe and labor.
  • Pipe material: PEX and copper differ in material cost and installation time.
  • Wall and ceiling access: Opening and repairing drywall is a major part of the job.
  • Permit and inspection: Work on the water line requires a village permit, and everything must meet code.
  • Scheduling and phasing: Doing the house in one visit or in stages changes the number of trips and the temporary work.

Insurance does not usually pay to replace old pipes because they are worn. It may cover resulting water damage if a pipe bursts suddenly, depending on the policy.

PEX versus copper

Choice of pipe is the biggest decision. Both are used in homes today. Neither is always better, and a good plumber should explain the trade-offs without pushing one by default.

Factor PEX Copper
Flexibility Bends around corners, fewer fittings Rigid, needs elbows and joints
Install time Usually faster Slower, soldering each joint
Wall openings Often fewer access holes Often more
Freeze tolerance Expands slightly, resists splitting Can split when frozen
Heat and sunlight Not for outdoor or sun exposure Tolerates both
Lifespan Long, but newer material Very long track record
Taste and water chemistry Some people note a plastic taste at first Depends on water chemistry

For most interior repipes, PEX lowers labor and drywall damage. Copper makes sense when a homeowner wants a traditional material or where lines will be exposed. Tap water in Morton Grove is moderately hard, so scale on fixtures will occur either way, and a water softener is a separate decision.

Drywall, access and the hidden cost

Opening walls is the part that surprises people. Pipes live in walls, ceilings and floors. To replace them, the plumber cuts access holes at each fixture and along the runs. After that, drywall repair and paint become separate trades.

Some plumbers include basic patching. Others leave the wall open. Ask who repairs and paints, because that line item can be larger than the plumbing for some homes. A house with a basement ceiling that is already open can be repiped with less damage than one finished everywhere.

Phasing the job

If you cannot be without water for long or cannot afford the whole job at once, ask about phasing. A phased repipe replaces one zone at a time, such as the kitchen and bath first, then the rest. It costs more in total labor and leaves a mix of old and new pipe in the meantime. The advantage is cash flow and less disruption. The disadvantage is a longer project with repeated visits.

How to compare repipe quotes fairly

Three quotes can look very different when each assumes something else. Use this list to put them side by side.

  1. Ask each plumber to state the pipe material and brand type.
  2. Confirm how many fixtures are included and which are excluded.
  3. Find out who pulls the permit and who handles inspection.
  4. Ask who repairs drywall, and to what finish level.
  5. Check that the quote lists shutoff valves and the water heater connection.
  6. Ask what the warranty covers, in writing.
  7. Verify the plumber's licence with IDPH before you sign.

The lowest quote is not always the cheapest outcome. A quote that leaves out drywall repair or permits may cost more in the end.

When repiping is not the answer

Some problems look like a pipe failure but are not. Low pressure can come from a failing pressure regulator, a partly closed valve or a clogged aerator. Discolored water can come from the water heater, not the pipes. A plumber can test pressure and look at samples of cut pipe to confirm what is happening.

A single leaking section can be replaced locally. That is repair work, and it should be priced as a repair. Ask what the plumber found that points to a full repipe, and ask to see it.

Morton Grove housing, lead and permits

Morton Grove note

The village had 2,010 residents in 1940 and 20,533 twenty years later. That postwar building boom is why many homes date from the 1940s to the 1960s, and the village programme for overhead sewers is aimed at homes built before 1965. If your house is from that period, its original supply pipes may be well past their planned life, so a plumber should inspect them.

On lead, Public Works posts an online inventory of service lines on its water quality page, listing the pipe material for each address. In 2024 testing, the 90th percentile was 12.4 ppb and 3 sample sites were over the 15 ppb action level. Call Public Works at (847) 470-5235 about your address, and let the tap run for a few minutes before drinking. A repipe inside the house does not replace the service line from the street.

Village rules call for a permit whenever a sewer or water line gets repaired. For a repipe, the building office on (847) 470-5214 can tell you how your project would be treated.
