Remodels and New Fixtures

Plumbing Installation in Morton Grove, IL

New fixtures, moved fixtures and rough-in work, installed by licensed plumbers with the village permit sorted before the walls close.

Call (847) 281-8626

Plumbing installation in Morton Grove means putting new supply lines, drains, vents and fixtures into a home or building, and connecting them to the existing system to code. It covers a bathroom remodel, a new kitchen sink, a basement bath, a laundry hookup or the rough-in for a house under construction. Our licensed plumbers do the work, and the clear price comes before work starts.

Most installations start as a plan, and the plan decides whether the job goes smoothly. A few questions up front, including whether a permit is needed, save weeks later.

Start with what is changing

Installing a fixture in the same place as an old one is straightforward. Moving a fixture, adding a new one, or creating a new bath is a bigger job. The pipes, drains and vents have to reach the new spot at the right slope, and walls, floors and joists may need to be opened.

That distinction drives the cost and the permit. A like-for-like swap of a faucet or toilet is usually maintenance. A new location is a relocation, and the village treats it differently.

Questions to settle before you hire

  • Which fixtures are new, and which are being replaced in place?
  • Will any fixture move to a different wall or floor location?
  • Is there a drain and vent nearby, or does a new line need to be run?
  • Is the project below the main sewer line, such as a basement bath?
  • Who is pulling the permit and scheduling the inspection?
  • What is the written scope, and what is left to other trades?

Permits and the Illinois Plumbing Code

A permit is needed in Morton Grove when you move a plumbing fixture, put in a water heater, or add a sewer cleanout, flood control valve or overhead sewer. Routine upkeep is exempt. The village says plan review rarely takes more than 15 business days, so leave that much room in a remodel calendar.

Local plumbing rules start from the 2014 Illinois Plumbing Code, which Morton Grove amended for its own use. That code sets pipe sizes, venting, drain slope and trap rules, and an inspector checks the work against it. If your project falls outside the permit list above, phone the village's Building and Inspectional Services desk on (847) 470-5214.

Morton Grove also licenses the contractors themselves. To get that licence a contractor must carry liability coverage and post a $10,000 bond, and it runs for 12 months. On top of that, state law requires plumbing installers to hold an IDPH licence, and you can look a name up on the IDPH site before you sign anything.

Fixture rough-in and remodels

The rough-in is the hidden stage of a remodel. It is the supply lines, drains and vents installed inside the walls and floors before drywall and tile cover them. Getting the positions exact matters, since a toilet flange set an inch off or a vent that does not reach the roof cannot be fixed after the tile is down.

On a bathroom remodel, the plumber confirms the measurements against the fixtures you bought. Toilet rough-in distance, shower valve depth and vanity drain height all vary by model. Bring the spec sheets to the planning visit and avoid buying anything unusual until the plumber has seen the layout.

A remodel is also a chance to fix what is behind the wall. If the pipes you open are corroded galvanized, ask about repiping the affected section while the wall is open, since it costs less than opening the same wall twice.

New fixtures: sinks, toilets, showers and laundry

Adding a fixture where a line already exists is the easiest case. A new sink in a kitchen island, a utility sink in a laundry room or an extra shower head in an existing bath all use nearby supply and drain.

For a new toilet, the drain must be large enough and correctly vented. If you are replacing an existing toilet, see toilet repair for the question of whether the old unit can be fixed instead. A toilet that keeps running or rocking is sometimes a seal, a flapper or a flange, and not a reason to replace the whole unit.

Water heaters are installed under their own permit rules. If your project includes a new tank or a tankless unit, the water heater installation page covers it. Every water heater needs a permit in Morton Grove, and the permit can be applied for online.

Basement baths, sewage ejectors and flooding risk

A basement bathroom adds a problem that upstairs baths do not have. The fixtures sit below the level of the sewer line in the street, so waste cannot drain by gravity. A sewage ejector pit collects waste and a pump lifts it up to the house drain.

The pit must be sealed and vented, the pump sized for the load, and the discharge connected correctly. A failed pump in a basement bath can back up into the room, so a high-water alarm and a reliable pump matter more here than anywhere else in the house.

Morton Grove also has a history of sewer flooding. Its assistance program for overhead sewers outlines two ways to stop sewage backing up, and it applies to homes built before 1965. If you are opening the basement floor for a new bath, ask about a sewer inspection first, since it shows the condition of the lateral that will carry the new fixtures. Roof downspouts, footing drains and driveway drains are not allowed to tie into the sanitary sewer.

New construction plumbing

New construction work is installed in stages. The first is the underground rough-in, where drains and vents are set before the slab or basement floor is poured. The second is the above-floor rough-in, once the framing is up. The final stage is trim, when fixtures and faucets are set.

Every stage is inspected before it is covered. A plumber who coordinates with the builder on the schedule avoids having to open a finished floor to correct a missed drain. If you are the owner-builder or a general contractor, ask for the permit, inspection and test schedule in writing.

What affects the cost of plumbing installation

  • Distance from existing lines: Moving a fixture far from its old supply and drain adds pipe and labor.
  • Access: Open framing or an unfinished basement is quicker than finished walls and tile.
  • Fixture type: Wall-hung toilets, freestanding tubs and multi-head showers need more rough-in work.
  • Permit and inspection: Plan review, permit paperwork and inspection visits are part of the schedule.
  • Extras like ejector pits: A pit, pump and venting add parts and concrete work.

Home insurance generally does not pay for planned remodels or new fixtures. It may respond if an installation fails and causes sudden water damage, so keep the permit, the inspection sign-off and the written scope. Compare quotes line by line, and expect the written scope to show the price before the first pipe is cut.
