A water heater that has split open cannot be repaired, and the water it releases will not wait for business hours. Emergency water heater replacement in Morton Grove, IL means a licensed plumber removes the failed tank and installs a new one to code. Phones are staffed day and night, and a same-day appointment is offered when the schedule allows. If your tank is leaking now, close the cold supply valve above it and switch off its power or gas.

Signs the tank is finished

  • Water seeping from the jacket, the bottom seam or the base of the tank
  • A puddle that returns after you dry it
  • Rust flakes or brown water from the hot tap
  • A heater that has needed several repairs in a short time
  • Loud banging or boiling sounds from heavy scale inside
  • Hot water that runs out far sooner than it used to

When a water heater tank floods the basement

A tank fails in three common ways. A slow seep starts as damp insulation and a rusty ring, and it can run for weeks before anyone notices. A seam failure releases water in a steady stream, and it empties most of the tank within the hour. A burned-out gas burner chamber or a scorched element tank may still hold water but is no longer safe to relight.

Seam failures are the true emergency. A full tank holds a lot of water, and finished basements, laundry rooms and carpeted utility closets take damage fast. After-hours calls go to the same dispatch line, and the first job is stopping the water before anything else.

What to do while you wait

  • Turn the shutoff on the cold line above the tank to closed, then open a hot tap upstairs so pressure in the tank drops.
  • On a gas unit, set the control knob to off. On an electric unit, trip the breaker only when you can get to the panel without crossing wet floor.
  • Gas odor in the room means getting everyone outside first. Then dial 911 and the utility emergency line listed on your Nicor Gas statement.
  • Move boxes, rugs and stored items off the floor. Do not wade through standing water near outlets.
  • Take photos of the tank, the label and the water damage for your records.

Replace or repair: deciding with the tank in front of you

Not every leak means a new heater. A leaking fitting, drain valve or relief valve is a repair, and a plumber should say so before quoting a swap. Water from the shell is different, because corrosion cannot be reversed. Our water heater repair page covers the faults that can be fixed.

What the plumber finds Usual verdict Why
Leak from a fitting or valve, tank sound Repair The shell is intact and the part is cheap to swap
Leak from the shell or bottom seam Replace Steel that has rusted through cannot be sealed
Old tank, failed thermostat or element Close call A new part may buy little time on a tired tank
Heavy sediment and a rusted burner chamber Replace Scale and rust shorten the life of what remains
Sound tank, no hot water, simple part fault Repair The tank still has service life left

Gas, electric, hybrid or tankless: choosing the next heater

Most homeowners replace like for like, because the gas line, vent or circuit is already in place. Switching fuel is possible but adds work. A move from gas to electric needs a dedicated circuit, and a move from electric to gas needs a gas line and a vent.

  • Gas tank: Lowest operating cost in many homes, needs a working vent and gas supply.
  • Electric tank: Simple to install, no venting, but needs a suitable circuit and costs more to run in many homes.
  • Heat pump hybrid: An electric tank with a heat pump on top. It uses less electricity, needs open air around it and a condensate drain, and is taller than a standard tank.
  • Tankless: Heats water as it flows, takes less floor space and can need a larger gas line and special venting. See tankless water heater for the trade-offs.

An emergency swap is easiest when the new heater matches the old one. If you want to change type, tell the dispatcher so the plumber arrives with the right parts and a realistic plan.

Who is allowed to replace your water heater

State law requires a licence for anyone who installs, alters or repairs plumbing. IDPH issues those licences, and the public can search them online by name or business. Before the old tank is touched, run the search yourself and compare the result with what the plumber has told you.

Morton Grove adds a second layer. A company doing jobs inside village limits must also carry the village's own contractor licence, backed by insurance and a bond. Ask any company, ours included, to show both the state licence and the village one before work begins.

What happens on replacement day

A typical emergency replacement

  1. 1
    Stop the waterThe plumber closes the supply, shuts off gas or power and drains the old tank, usually through a hose to a floor drain or outdoors.
  2. 2
    Check the surroundingsThe vent, gas line, water connectors, drain pan and shutoff valves are inspected, since a new heater on worn fittings invites another leak.
  3. 3
    Disconnect and removeThe old tank comes out and leaves with the crew.
  4. 4
    Set and connectThe new heater is placed, leveled and connected to water, fuel and venting, with a new relief valve discharge pipe and an expansion tank when the system calls for one.
  5. 5
    Fill, test and startThe tank is filled and purged of air before power or gas goes on. The plumber checks flame or element operation and looks for leaks at every joint.

What moves the price of a new water heater

What changes the cost of a replacement

  • Type of heater: A standard tank, a hybrid and a tankless unit differ greatly in equipment cost.
  • Size: A larger household needs a larger tank or a higher-output tankless unit.
  • Venting and gas line: Old flues, a changed fuel type or a longer gas run add work.
  • Code upgrades: A missing expansion tank, shutoff valve or discharge pipe has to be added.
  • Permit and access: The permit step and a hard-to-reach location both add time.

Homeowners insurance may pay for water damage from a sudden tank failure but generally does not pay for the new heater itself. Ask your carrier before cleanup begins, and keep photos and the old tank's label.
