Skokie is large, at 10.07 square miles, so it helps to think about it in corridors. The sections below follow the main roads and rail stops, then the problems that turn up along them.

The Edens side of Skokie

The Edens Expressway crosses the west side of Skokie, and that edge of the village is the part closest to Morton Grove. Give the nearest cross street and which side of the expressway you are on when you call. That tells dispatch which way to approach, since the Edens is a hard line on the map.

A main sewer line carries every drain in the house to the street connection. Sewer line cleaning clears that pipe with a heavy cable or a high-pressure water jet, and it is the usual fix when several fixtures slow down together. If cleaning keeps failing, a camera inspection shows whether roots or a collapsed section are the real cause.

Dempster Street and Oakton Street

Dempster Street and Oakton Street are the main east and west roads through the village, and both lead back toward Morton Grove. Any building on these roads with more than one unit, such as a storefront with apartments above, usually shares a drain stack. One tenant's clog can then show up in another unit's floor drain.

If that happens, tell the property owner at once. A stack clog cannot be cleared from a single sink, and plunging only pushes water toward the lowest unit. A plumber can open the stack at a cleanout and run a cable from there.

Signs the main line is the problem

  • Gurgling in a tub or toilet when the washing machine drains
  • Slow drains in more than one room at once
  • A floor drain that bubbles or smells
  • Water backing up into the lowest fixture in the home
  • Repeat clogs that return within weeks of cabling

Around Westfield Old Orchard and Old Orchard Road

Westfield Old Orchard and Old Orchard Road mark the retail side of the village, and Skokie Boulevard and Golf Road both lead toward it. Tell dispatch the nearest of these roads when you call, so the plumber knows which direction to come from.

A supply pipe inside a wall can leak for weeks before a stain shows. Leak detection pairs sound-based listening gear with a pressure test to pin down the exact spot. Thermal imaging can also reveal a warm hot-water line running under a floor. That means a smaller hole in the wall than guessing would cause.

Near the Yellow Line and Oakton Street station

The CTA Yellow Line ends at Dempster Street, and the Oakton Street station opened in 2012. If you live in a multi-unit building near either stop, a leak in one unit can drip into the ceiling of the one below. The first call goes to the building manager, then to a plumber.

When water seeps through your ceiling, close the supply to your own unit and stay away from any light fixture it reaches. A ceiling holding water can drop without warning, so stay out from under a bulging spot.

When pipes burst in a Skokie winter

Skokie winters can freeze unheated pipes in garages, crawl spaces and exterior walls. A frozen pipe often shows as a trickle or no water from one tap. Leave the tap open, keep the room warm, and shut the main valve if you see a crack, because ice that melts can turn a small split into a spray. For splits that have already opened, see burst pipe repair.

Homeownership in Skokie is about 74.7 percent, so a large share of calls come from tenants in rented units. If you rent, photograph the damage before you move anything and send those pictures to your landlord with the time you called.

Skokie note

Skokie was incorporated in 1888 as Niles Centre and renamed Skokie by referendum in 1940.

It buys its water wholesale from the City of Evanston, so it receives the same Lake Michigan source as Morton Grove. Evanston's plant has a capacity of 108 million gallons per day and no sewage plant. Wastewater goes to the Metropolitan Water Reclamation District's North Side plant, so a blocked line on your property is your plumber's job, while a problem in the street main starts with the village.

Before we arrive in Skokie

Quick moves that limit damage until a plumber is at the door:

  • Find the main shutoff and close it if water is running freely.
  • Stop all water use if sewage is rising in a drain, and keep children and pets away.
  • Kill the breaker for a soaked room only if you can get to the panel without stepping in water.
  • A gas odor means everyone goes outside. Dial 911 and the gas company from the yard or the street.
  • Photograph the damage before cleanup, since your insurer or landlord will want it.
