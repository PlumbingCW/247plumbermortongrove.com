Clogged drains come first

A drain clog is a blockage in a fixture trap or a branch line that stops water from leaving. One slow sink is usually hair, soap or grease sitting in the trap. When the tub, the toilet and the floor drain all gurgle at once, the blockage is deeper, in the main line that carries everything out of the house.

Drain cleaning starts with a cable machine that cuts or pulls the clog out, with the cable size matched to the pipe. When grease or roots have coated the pipe wall, hydro jetting scours it with high-pressure water. Jetting costs more than cabling, but it leaves the pipe cleaner and the clog is less likely to return.

You can plunge a single fixture. Skip the bottles of caustic cleaner, because they sit in standing water and can burn skin and soften old pipes. Call when two or more fixtures are slow together, since that points to the main line.

Water heaters that fail on a cold morning

A tank water heater stores about 40 to 50 gallons of hot water, and when it fails you notice at the first shower. The common causes are a dead pilot or ignition part on a gas unit, a burned-out element on an electric one, or a tank that has started to leak. Water heater repair fits the first two. A leaking tank cannot be patched, so it means replacement.

Rumbling or popping from the tank usually points to scale on the bottom. Niles drinks Lake Michigan water that the city of Evanston treats and sends through the commission Morton Grove also belongs to. That water measured about 8 grains per gallon of hardness in the 2023 report, which is moderate. Over many years it builds scale in the tank, so a noisy old heater is a better replacement candidate than one that only lost its pilot.

A gas odor near the heater changes everything. Walk outside first, phone 911 and the utility number printed on your bill, and only then ring us. Leave light switches and appliances alone on the way out.

Signs your water heater needs a plumber

  • No hot water at all, or hot water that runs out in a few minutes
  • Water pooling around the base of the tank
  • Rumbling, popping or hissing from inside the tank
  • Rusty or cloudy hot water, with clear cold water
  • A pilot flame that keeps going out

Leaks you can hear but cannot find

A hidden leak is water escaping from a pipe inside a wall, slab or ceiling where you cannot see it. The clues are a water meter that keeps moving with every fixture off, a damp patch on a ceiling, or a bill that climbs for no reason. With leak detection, the plumber listens along the pipe with sensitive microphones and then pressurizes the line to confirm the spot.

That means one small opening in the drywall instead of a torn-out wall.

Shut the main valve if you can reach it. Then check the meter again after a few minutes, because a stopped meter tells the plumber the leak is on the house side of the line.

Sewer backups and what to do first

A sewer backup is wastewater rising into the house through the lowest opening, whether a basement floor drain, a tub or a toilet bowl, rather than flowing away. Treat it as a health problem. Keep children and pets away, stop running water, and do not touch the water without gloves and boots.

If the backup is only in the lowest drain, the problem is usually the line between your house and the street. A camera inspection shows whether it is a clog, a root intrusion or a broken section, and sewer line repair is the next step when the pipe itself has failed.
