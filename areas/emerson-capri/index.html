Emerson and Capri is a residential pocket of Morton Grove, centered on Emerson Street and Capri Lane. Real estate listing sources place the Capri Cove subdivision south of Golf Road and west of Harms Road, with single-family homes built from 1955 to 1957. Those are broker descriptions, and the village has not published a boundary, so treat the streets as a guide and not a border.

What sets this area apart for a plumber is its flood record. The Cook County hazard plan lists "the National Park and Emerson/Capri neighborhoods" among Morton Grove areas susceptible to flooding. The plan does not name houses, causes or how often water arrives. It is a reason to take basement protection seriously, and not a prediction for any one home.

Why basements get the attention here

Morton Grove has had several heavy-rain events in the plan, including 13 September 2006, 22 July 2011 and 18 April 2013, which brought 4.8 inches and a federal disaster declaration. In a storm like that, water reaches a basement in two ways. It seeps through the floor and walls, or sewage backs up through the lowest drain.

The two problems need different fixes. A sump pump handles groundwater that collects under the slab. Backup prevention handles sewage that comes up the house sewer. Many homes need both, and a plumber can tell which one failed by where the water appeared first.

Signs your basement needs attention

  • Water at the floor drain or the lowest toilet during a storm
  • A sump pit that fills but does not empty
  • A pump that runs constantly or makes grinding noises
  • Damp walls or a musty smell after rain
  • Gurgling drains when the washer or a shower runs

Homes built before 1965

The village runs an Overhead Sewer and Flood Control Assistance Program for owner-occupied homes built before 1965. Homes of that era use gravity sewer connections, which are more open to sanitary backups in long, heavy rain. If the listing dates for this area are right, many houses here fall before that cutoff.

Do not assume. The village Building and Inspectional Services desk, at (847) 470-5214, can confirm the year built and whether the program is open this year. Funding and rules can change. The program asks for three written bids from Illinois-licensed contractors, so collect them before you commit. Our sump pump work and sewer line repair pages explain both approaches in plain terms.

What a plumber looks at first

On a flooding call, the plumber checks where the water entered. A wet floor drain points to the sewer side. A full sump pit points to the pump, its discharge line or a failed check valve. The plumber also confirms that footing drains and downspouts are not tied to the sanitary sewer, which village code does not allow. Municipal Code 7-5-4A requires them to drain to a separate storm system such as a sump.

Permits come into it as well. Flood control valves, overhead sewers, sewer or water line repairs and new water heaters need one from the village, while routine cleaning does not. Settle who files the paperwork before the work begins.

Everyday plumbing for the area

Not every call is a flood. Older homes also need drain cleaning, faucet and toilet repairs, and water heater replacement when a tank wears out. Morton Grove tap water is Lake Michigan water that Evanston treats, and Evanston's 2023 report puts hardness near 8 grains per gallon. That is moderate, so scale builds slowly in a tank, and a water heater that rumbles after many years is a candidate for water heater replacement.

For a stubborn clog that keeps returning, a camera sewer inspection shows whether the cause is roots, grease or a damaged section. If you are buying a home here, it is worth doing before closing.

While you wait for the plumber

  • Pause the washer, dishwasher and every tap.
  • Keep kids and pets away from standing water.
  • Stay out of water that touches outlets, the panel or appliances.
  • A gas smell means leaving the house and phoning 911 first.
  • Lift boxes and electronics off the floor only if it is safe.
