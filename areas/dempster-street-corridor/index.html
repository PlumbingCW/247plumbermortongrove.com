Dempster Street is the main east-west road through Morton Grove and part of US 58. The village names it one of its two principal commercial corridors, and a Dempster Street Corridor Plan is underway. This page is for the businesses and nearby homes along it, and it is a commercial district, not a residential subdivision.

Our own base is on Dempster Street, so this is the stretch we know best. The corridor runs from the Edens Expressway area west toward the forest preserve and Harlem Avenue. The village's own plan describes small shops and businesses along it, on a busy state-controlled road.

What a busy commercial street asks of plumbing

A shop or office has the same pipes a house has, used harder and by more people. Restrooms get flushed all day, floor drains take mop water, and a water heater feeds staff and customers. When one fails, the cost is lost business, not just repair. For a food business, drain lines and grease control matter most. For an office, the restroom group and a shared water heater do.

Renters should start with ownership of the problem. Tenants in a strip building often feed one drain line, so a clog next door can surface in your sink. Check the lease for who maintains that line. A property manager who names one call contact saves everyone a scramble. Our commercial plumbing page covers the service in more detail.

Common commercial calls on a corridor like this

  • Floor drains that back up or drain slowly
  • Grease-laden kitchen lines that clog again within weeks
  • Restroom fixtures that leak, run or will not flush
  • A commercial water heater that stops mid-service
  • Leaks showing up as stains, a high water bill or a meter that keeps moving

Water on Dempster Street itself

The Cook County hazard plan lists several Dempster stretches as susceptible to flooding. They include Olcott to Harlem, the area by the North Branch of the Chicago River, the part near the forest preserve, and the section between Ozark Avenue and Waukegan Road. The plan also proposes a relief sewer with underground storage there, led by IDOT, to reduce closures of US 58 and basement flooding. It is a proposed action, so do not count on it.

For a business, the lesson is practical. If your building sits on one of those stretches, a floor drain or restroom in a basement or low level is the likely place for water to show up. A backflow valve and a working sump are worth asking about. Our sump pump repair and drain cleaning pages explain the options.

Permits and who may do the work

Paperwork depends on the job. Replacing a water heater or repairing a sewer or water line takes a village permit, and so does relocating a fixture. Routine upkeep does not. Anyone doing contract work inside village limits must carry the village's own contractor licence, so it is fair to ask for proof. If you are unsure, the Building and Inspectional Services desk can answer at (847) 470-5214.

Some properties also have backflow devices that need periodic testing. Ask the village whether yours do and how often, then book a plumber licensed for that work.

If a drain backs up in a shop or restaurant

  • Close the affected restrooms and kitchen sinks.
  • Block off the wet floor so no one slips.
  • Skip drain chemicals, which can splash staff.
  • Find the main water valve and shut it for a leaking pipe.
  • Smell gas? Get everyone out and phone 911 before anything else.
