Waukegan Road corridor

Plumber on Waukegan Road, Morton Grove, IL

Plumbing for the mix of homes, condominium buildings and businesses along Waukegan Road, south from Dempster Street.

Waukegan Road is the village's other main commercial corridor, alongside Dempster Street. Village planning documents describe it as running from Dempster Street south to Caldwell Avenue, with commercial, residential and industrial uses side by side. That mix is the reason this page exists. The plumbing on Waukegan Road is as likely to serve a condo stack or a small industrial unit as a single house.

The Sawmill Station retail area sits where Waukegan Road meets Dempster Street. Road improvements finished in 2001 added a turn lane, sidewalks and streetlights. Waukegan is a state-controlled arterial, so busy hours can slow a visit.

Shared plumbing is the main issue

Many buildings along a mixed-use road share plumbing. Listing sources place several condominium communities on or near Waukegan Road south of Dempster. In a condo or apartment building, units often share vertical drain stacks and a building sewer. Water heaters may be shared or individual, and the owners' association decides who pays for what.

That changes the first step of a repair. If one unit has a clog, the problem may be inside the unit or in the shared line. If several units back up together, the shared line is the likely cause. Tell the plumber how many fixtures and units are affected, and tell the board or manager at the same time. Our sewer line cleaning page explains how a main line is cleared.

Questions to settle before you call

  • Is the problem in one unit or in several?
  • Does the association or the owner maintain that pipe?
  • Who must approve a repair on a shared line?
  • Is a permit needed, and who files it?
  • Is there an after-hours contact for the building?

Homes, shops and light industry together

A single-family house, a shop and a small industrial unit can sit within a block of each other here. Each needs something different. A house needs drains, fixtures and a reliable water heater. A shop needs restrooms and floor drains that work all day. An industrial user may need floor drains, and often a separate trap or interceptor that must be kept clean.

The village's Industrial Areas Plan, adopted in 2014, covers its industrial districts, but it does not say where each one is. If you operate a workshop or warehouse on the corridor, ask Building and Inspectional Services at (847) 470-5214 what your use requires. Water, leaks and the lowest level

The Cook County hazard plan lists Dempster Street between Ozark Avenue and Waukegan Road as susceptible to flooding. It also proposes a relief sewer there to reduce closures of US 58. If your building sits near that corner, a basement drain or ground-floor restroom is where water would show up first. Check your drains after heavy rain and keep the area around floor drains clear.

A leak in a mixed-use building can run for a while before anyone sees it. Warning signs are a water meter that keeps moving, a stained ceiling below a restroom, or a rising bill for the building. Our leak detection service finds a hidden leak without opening more wall than needed.

Before the plumber arrives

  • Hold off on showers, flushing and laundry while drains are backing up.
  • Warn neighbors in a shared building so they stop running water.
  • Close the main water valve for a pipe that keeps leaking.
  • Keep away from wet floors around outlets and panels.
  • A gas smell means everyone out, then 911.

Permits and licensing

In a condo or mixed building, the permit question has a second layer. The village issues the permit, but the association may also need to approve the work before it begins. Ask the manager first, then ask the plumber who applies. Water heaters, sewer or water line repairs and fixture moves all fall under the village's permit rules.
