Spring storms and the sump pump

Spring brings heavy rain, and the basement is the first place it shows. A sump pump sits in a pit in the lowest part of the floor and pushes groundwater outside before it can spread. When the pump fails, the pit overflows and the floor floods within hours. Sump pump repair and installation covers testing the float switch, replacing a worn pump and adding a battery backup for power cuts during storms.

Test yours before the first big storm. Pour a bucket of water into the pit and watch for the pump to start and drain it. If it stays silent, hums without moving water or runs nonstop, call a plumber before the sky turns dark.

Glenview Creek drains the southeast corner of the village into the Middle Fork of the North Branch of the Chicago River. Homes near that corner should treat heavy rain as a prompt to check the pump early, since low ground near a creek tends to hold more groundwater.

Summer heat, sprinklers and fixtures

Summer is the season for outdoor spigots, sprinkler lines and dishwashers that run all day. A hose bib that drips behind a wall can wet the framing for weeks before anyone notices. Check outside faucets for drips and look inside the cabinet below the kitchen sink for any dampness.

Hot weather also hides water heater problems, because nobody notices lukewarm showers in July. A heater that takes too long to recover may have scale in the tank. Water heater repair deals with failed thermostats, elements and gas valves, and a plumber flushes the tank at the same visit if sediment is the cause.

Glenview's water comes from the Village of Wilmette, which draws it from Lake Michigan. The 2018 Glenview report lists hardness at 150 mg/L, about 8.2 grains per gallon. Water at that level is only moderately hard, but scale still coats heating elements as the years pass and a tank that is flushed less often wears out sooner. Water softener installation is the long-term answer for homes that see crusty faucets and short heater life.

Summer symptoms worth a call

  • A water bill that rises with no change in habits
  • Damp drywall below a hose bib or sink
  • Lukewarm water that used to run hot
  • White crust on faucet aerators and shower heads
  • A tank that rumbles when it heats

Fall prep before the freeze

Fall is cheap insurance season. Disconnect garden hoses, shut and drain exterior lines if your house has a separate valve for them, and look at the water heater for rust at the base. If you have a gas heater, confirm the flue and venting look right and that no scorch marks show around the burner door.

The best time to fix a slow-draining tub or a sweating pipe is before the first hard frost, since a cold snap makes every fix urgent and every plumber busy. A leaking joint found in October is a short visit. The same leak found in January may be a flood.

Winter freezes and burst pipes

Cook County winters can freeze any pipe that runs through an unheated space. The first sign is a trickle or no water from one tap. Open it, keep the room warm and leave the cabinet doors under the sink open so room heat reaches the pipe. If you see a split or water sprays, close the main valve and call. Burst pipe repair means cutting out the damaged section and replacing it.

Never use an open flame to thaw a pipe. A hair dryer on a low setting at the cold spot is safe, and a heating pad wrapped on the pipe also works.

Where Glenview's history and ZIPs meet the plumbing

Glenview was farmland until after World War II, and residents voted on the name Glenview on 7 May 1895. The village was incorporated in 1899. Because it grew in stages, the age and pipe material of a house depend on the block, so ask the plumber to check what you have instead of assuming.

The Naval Air Station Glenview closed in 1995 and its 1,121 acres became The Glen. The Grove, a 123-acre prairie and National Historic Landmark, and the Kohl Children's Museum are both in the village, and either is a good landmark when you give directions. The Glenview and North Glenview Metra stations on the Milwaukee District North Line, the same line that serves Morton Grove, make good reference points too.

Glenview note

Glenview has 48,705 residents on 14.04 square miles, with a median home value of $609,600 and homeownership of 78.9 percent. With values that high, a repair that goes wrong can cost far more than the repair itself, so ask any plumber for the licence number and check it with IDPH before work starts. Glenview buys water from the Village of Wilmette, so water quality questions go to Wilmette or the village, not to your plumber.

Before we arrive in Glenview

Do these first, in this order. Each takes a minute or less.

  • Turn off the main water shutoff when a pipe has split or the tank is leaking.
  • Unplug the sump pump only if the cord is dry and you are standing on a dry surface.
  • Smell gas? Leave the house, then call 911 and the gas utility number on your bill.
  • Keep everyone out of a flooded basement until the power is off or confirmed safe.
  • Photograph the damage and the date, because your insurance carrier will ask.
