Faucets that drip, spray or will not shut off

A faucet works by sealing water behind a cartridge, washer or ceramic disc. When that seal wears, you get a drip. A drip of one drop per second can waste several gallons a day, and it also stains the basin. Faucet repair means replacing the worn part or the whole valve body, and the right choice depends on how old the faucet is and whether parts are still made for it.

Shut the small stop valve under the sink if a faucet will not turn off. The valve is a small oval handle on the water line. Turn it clockwise until it stops. If it does not hold, close the main valve.

Faucet signs to act on

  • A drip that continues after you turn the handle off hard
  • Water at the base of the spout or under the handle
  • Low flow from only one faucet in the house
  • A handle that is loose, stiff or grinds when turned
  • Rust or mineral crust on the aerator

Toilets that run, clog or leak at the base

A toilet is a simple machine with a tank that fills, a flapper that lets water drop into the bowl and a trap that holds the seal. A toilet that runs for minutes after a flush usually has a worn flapper or fill valve. Toilet repair swaps those parts, and the fix is quick when the porcelain is sound.

Water at the base is different. It points to a failed wax ring or a cracked bowl, and the floor under it may already be wet. Stop using the toilet, shut the small valve behind it and call.

When the leak is not at the fixture

Sometimes a damp ceiling or a patch of wet drywall points away from the fixture you suspect. Leak detection combines listening devices with pressure checks on the pipes, which shows where the water really escapes. The repair then opens only the one spot that matters.

A clog is a separate issue. If the sink and tub drain slowly at the same time, a plumber needs to cable the branch or main line. That is a drain cleaning job, not a faucet or toilet fix.

Before we arrive in Lincolnwood

Until the plumber is at your door, these moves keep the damage small:

  • Close the small stop valve at the fixture, or the main valve if water keeps flowing.
  • Mop or soak up standing water so it does not reach the subfloor.
  • Move rugs and cabinet contents out of the wet area.
  • Stay clear of outlets and appliances that are touching water.
  • Notice a gas smell? Get everyone outside before you dial 911 and the gas company.
