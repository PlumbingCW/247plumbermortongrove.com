Signs your water heater needs attention

  • Water pooling at the base or running down the side of the tank
  • Dripping from the pipes or connectors on top
  • Rust-colored water from the hot tap only
  • Rumbling, popping or ticking from inside the tank
  • Hot water that runs out within a few minutes
  • A pilot light that keeps going out, or a burner flame that looks weak and yellow
  • Steady water from the pipe attached to the relief valve

When a leaking water heater is an emergency

A slow weep from a fitting can wait for a morning appointment. Water running from the tank seam or pouring out of the relief valve pipe is an active failure, and it will not stop on its own. If the water is spreading across a finished floor, toward a floor drain or near the electrical panel, treat it as urgent and call at any hour.

A heater that gives out at 2 a.m. in January leaves a household with no hot water and sometimes a wet basement. Night and weekend calls go to the same dispatch line as daytime calls. The plumber deals with the water first and the hot water second.

What to do while you wait

  • Find the shutoff on the cold pipe entering the top of the tank and turn the handle fully closed. A stopped supply keeps the leak from refilling.
  • Gas heater: rotate the control knob to off. Electric heater: flip its breaker off, but only when the floor around the panel is dry and the panel is easy to reach.
  • A rotten-egg smell means everyone goes outside. From there, phone 911 and the gas emergency line printed on your Nicor Gas statement, and leave light switches alone.
  • If water is touching outlets, cords or the panel, stay clear and tell the dispatcher.
  • Keep children and pets out of the water. Water from a failed tank can scald.

Tank leak or fitting leak: how to tell

Plumbers sort leaks into two groups, and the group decides the whole job. Fitting leaks come from supply connectors, the drain valve, the relief valve or the heating element gasket. Tank leaks come from inside the steel shell after corrosion has eaten through it.

Dry every wet spot with a towel, wait ten minutes, then watch where the first bead of water returns. Water that comes back from underneath the jacket almost always means the tank has failed. If the puddle does not match any spot above, the water may be coming from a hidden line nearby, and leak detection can trace it with acoustic listening and a moisture meter.

Gas, electric and hybrid heaters fail in different ways

The fuel type tells the plumber where to look first. The same symptom, no hot water, can come from three very different faults.

Gas heaters: pilot, thermocouple and burner

A gas heater keeps a small flame or an electronic igniter lit. The thermocouple is a probe sitting in that flame, and it tells the gas valve the flame is on. When the probe wears out, the valve shuts the gas and the pilot goes dark. The plumber tests it, cleans the burner and checks the vent draft.

Nicor Gas serves Morton Grove, so any gas smell goes to 911 and the utility's emergency number before anything else.

Electric heaters: elements and thermostats

An electric tank has an upper and a lower heating element, each with its own thermostat. A dead upper element means no hot water at all. A dead lower element means hot water that runs out fast, which is the most common complaint. The plumber tests each part with a meter, so the answer comes in minutes.

Heat pump hybrids

A hybrid heater adds a compressor and fan on top of an electric tank. It has more ways to fail, such as a clogged condensate drain, a dirty air filter or a sensor fault. Many hybrids fall back to their electric elements when the heat pump stops, so the tank still makes warm water while the top section sits idle. Parts supply varies by manufacturer, and the plumber will say plainly whether repair is sensible.

Why sediment and the anode rod decide how long a tank lasts

Morton Grove tap water comes from Evanston, and that city's 2023 report puts average hardness near 136 mg/L, or roughly 8.0 grains per gallon. The water is moderately hard. Dissolved minerals settle out as sediment on the tank floor, right where the burner or lower element does its work. Sediment causes the rumbling, wastes fuel and overheats the steel under it. On a Morton Grove tank, a yearly flush is worth doing.

The anode rod is a sacrificial metal rod that corrodes so the tank steel does not. Once it is gone, the shell starts to rust. Checking the rod during a repair visit tells the plumber how much life is left, and that feeds the repair-or-replace answer. If scale keeps coming back, a water softener is an option, though at this hardness it is a choice and not a requirement.

No hot water, or water that never gets hot

Total loss of hot water usually means the heater lost power, lost its flame or lost an element. Check the breaker, or look for a lit pilot on a gas unit, and press the red reset button once if your model has one.

If the breaker trips again or the pilot will not stay lit, stop and call, because repeated resets can hide a wiring or gas fault. Lukewarm water points to a failed lower element, a cracked dip tube that mixes cold water in, or heavy sediment.

What the plumber does on a repair visit

A typical water heater repair visit

  1. 1
    Make it safeThe plumber shuts off gas or power and the cold supply, then checks for gas leaks and wet wiring.
  2. 2
    Find the sourceThe tank is dried and the water is traced to a fitting, a valve or the shell.
  3. 3
    Test the partsThermostats, elements, the thermocouple, the relief valve and the anode rod are checked as they apply.
  4. 4
    Set out the optionsYou hear the findings and a clear price before work starts.
  5. 5
    Repair and testThe part is replaced, the tank is refilled and purged of air, and the heater runs a full cycle while the plumber watches for drips.

What moves the price of a water heater repair

What changes the cost of a repair

  • The failed part: A thermostat, an element, a relief valve and a gas control valve differ widely in part cost and labor.
  • Tank age and condition: A heater with a corroded shell or a spent anode rod may not be worth a new part.
  • Fuel type: Gas units add venting and combustion checks that electric units skip.
  • Access: A tank in a tight closet or at the bottom of a steep basement stair takes longer to work on.
  • Parts on hand: A part the plumber carries can be fitted in one visit, while a special order adds a second trip.

Homeowners insurance often covers sudden water damage from a failed heater, but it usually does not pay for the heater itself, and wear is typically excluded. Photograph the damage before cleanup and read your policy before you call the carrier.

Repair or replace: where the line sits

A tank that leaks through the shell cannot be patched, so repair stops there. A heater well past its first decade that needs a major part is a close call, because the next failure is likely to follow soon after.
