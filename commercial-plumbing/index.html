When a business plumbing problem is an emergency

Commercial plumbing carries more consequences than a house does. A leak can ruin inventory, close a kitchen or trip a health code. Some situations need a call at any hour.

Call right away if sewage is backing up through floor drains or toilets, if water is spreading through a ceiling, or if a water heater or pipe is leaking over electrical equipment. Hot water failure in a restaurant or salon also qualifies, since the business can hardly run without it.

Contain it before the plumber arrives

  • Shut the building's main water valve and any local shutoff for the affected fixture
  • If water reaches outlets, panels or equipment, keep away from it and cut power at the panel only when the floor around it is dry
  • Keep staff and customers away from sewage, which is a health hazard
  • Move stock, paper and electronics out of the wet area
  • Photograph the damage and the source for your insurer and, if you rent, for your landlord
  • If you smell gas, leave the building, call 911 and call the gas utility from outside

Floor drains and drain overflows

Floor drains in kitchens, back rooms and mechanical spaces collect spills, equipment drainage and, in some buildings, rainwater. When they back up, the cause is usually grease, debris, a collapsed line or a blockage in the main drain serving the whole building.

A plumber starts by checking whether one drain is slow or several are. Several affected drains suggest the main is the problem, and the fix is a larger cable or hydro jetting, which uses water at high pressure to scour the pipe walls. One slow drain can be a local blockage that drain cleaning clears. A camera inspection then shows whether the pipe is broken or just coated.

Grease traps and interceptors

Restaurants, cafes and food counters rely on grease traps to keep fats out of the sewer. A trap that is full of grease stops working. Fat then passes into the drain line and hardens, and the kitchen backs up during the dinner rush.

Trap cleaning is a scheduled job, and an overdue trap is the most common reason a kitchen floods on a busy night. Pumping removes the waste, and the plumber inspects baffles, gaskets and lids while the trap is empty. Ask how often your trap should be cleaned, since that depends on volume, and keep your service record handy for any inspector.

Signs a commercial drain system is in trouble

  • Several floor drains slow down or gurgle at once
  • Toilets bubble when sinks drain
  • Foul smell near a grease trap or floor drain
  • Water rises in the mop sink when a dishwasher runs
  • Standing water that is slow to leave after a normal day
  • A wet spot on the slab that never dries

Leak detection in commercial buildings

A hidden leak in a commercial building shows up as a rising water bill, a stain, or a wet slab with no visible source. Finding it before opening walls matters, because a blind guess means a damaged ceiling or a cut floor.

Leak detection uses acoustic listening, pressure testing and thermal imaging to narrow down the source. Not every suspected leak turns out to be one, since condensation and irrigation can also cause stains. The plumber will tell you if the evidence does not point to a leak, rather than open the ceiling to find out.

Backflow testing

A backflow preventer is a valve assembly that stops dirty water from running backward into the clean supply. Businesses with irrigation, fire sprinklers, boilers or food service often have one. These devices need testing, and the schedule depends on the type of device and the requirement of the water supplier.

A licensed plumber can test the assembly, record the result and replace worn parts. Since the testing rules vary, the village building office is the place to learn what applies to your property. Keep the test report on file for any inspector.

Hot water for commercial use

A failed water heater in an office is a nuisance, but in a restaurant or salon it stops the day. Commercial units run harder and often need a different size or type than residential ones. Where a unit is past repair, a water heater replacement can be planned for off-peak hours so the business loses less time.

Operating on Dempster, Waukegan and around Sawmill Station

Many of the shops and restaurants in Morton Grove sit along Dempster Street and Waukegan Road, and the Sawmill Station retail area sits where those roads meet. In any building where tenants share a drain line, one tenant's grease problem can back up the next unit. Ask your landlord who maintains the shared line before you pay for a repair that is not yours.

Property managers and landlords can set up a standing contact so tenants know who to call. Commercial work in the village also calls for a plumber with a municipal contractor licence, which comes with liability insurance and a $10,000 bond. Ask to see the paper before work begins.

After-hours and same-day commercial service

Emergency calls are taken at every hour, and a same-day visit can be arranged if the day has room. For a scheduled job like trap cleaning or backflow testing, early mornings and closing hours keep disruption low.

What moves the price of commercial plumbing work

  • Scope and building size: a single clogged drain differs from a main line serving a whole building
  • Access and hours: work in a busy kitchen or after closing needs scheduling and sometimes extra labour
  • Equipment needed: jetting, camera work or a backflow test each use different gear
  • Code and permit needs: sewer and water line repairs, and some fixture changes, require a permit
  • Hour of the visit: a late-night or holiday call can cost more than a daytime one, and the difference is named before work begins

Morton Grove note

A contractor who works inside village limits must hold a Village Contractor's License, which is valid for 12 months and tied to liability insurance and a $10,000 bond.
